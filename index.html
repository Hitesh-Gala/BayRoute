BayRoute

A study of the future of warfare, in the Indian context.

A joint project of Takshashila Institution, ideaForge and Drone Federation India.

Lead institution Takshashila Institution An independent centre for research and education in public policy, based in Bengaluru. Industry partner ideaForge A pioneer of Indian unmanned aerial systems for defence, security and industry. Industry partner Drone Federation India The industry body for India's drone and counter-drone sector. The project Project BayRoute Forty conversations with people who have commanded, advised and negotiated for India.

How will India's next wars be fought?

BayRoute puts that question to the people best placed to answer it, and asks whether the country is preparing for the answer.

The project will hold long-form conversations with India's serving and former military chiefs, with commanders and soldier-scholars, with those who have run and advised the national security apparatus, and with diplomats, scholars and political leaders. Every conversation follows a common core of questions, so that views can be compared across services, institutions and generations, alongside questions drawn from each person's own experience.

The timing is deliberate. Operation Sindoor, the war in Ukraine and the conflicts in West Asia have shown how quickly drones, precision weapons, space and software are changing the character of war. India meets these changes with two nuclear-armed neighbours, unsettled borders and a defence industry still finding its scale.

A parallel to Project Meridian

In September 2026 the United States commissioned Project Meridian, a 120-day study of the technologies and capabilities its forces will need over the next two decades. BayRoute pursues a similar question for India, but deliberately spans strategy, institutions and politics as well as technology.

On the name

BayRoute borrows its name, with affection, from a restaurant in Powai, Mumbai. Serious strategic conversations have often begun around a table.

How the study works

  1. Listen

    Structured, long-form interviews with about forty people across eight communities, each built on a shared core of questions.

  2. Compare

    Answers are read side by side to find where India's military, diplomatic and political establishments agree, and where they do not.

  3. Share

    Findings are drawn together to inform public debate and policy on how India prepares for future war.
